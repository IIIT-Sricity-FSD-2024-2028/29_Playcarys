0 · The verdict, up front

Stop building this first

Your Revenue Model document, stream 04, says: "Tournaments — entry fee and rake… Allow entry in Carys or cash… Platform takes 10-15%." Stream 02 says: "players buy Carys with money."

Under the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 — in force 1 May 2026 — that exact structure is a banned "online money game." Section 5 prohibits it absolutely. Section 9(1) makes it punishable by up to 3 years imprisonment and ₹1 crore fine. Section 10 makes it cognizable and non-bailable. Section 5 binds "no person" — it reaches you personally, not just a company you might later form.

Skill versus chance is expressly irrelevant under the Act. There is no threshold, no student exemption, and no small-platform carve-out. Chess being a game of skill does not help you.

The three things that follow from that, in order of how much they should change your plan:

  1. The tournament rake model is dead in India. Not risky — illegal. And even if you convinced yourself otherwise, you could not collect the money: Razorpay's terms clause 14.10 explicitly prohibits money games as defined under this exact Act, and Stripe prohibits "tournaments or competitions… with a monetary or material prize." The payment rails are a harder wall than the statute.
  2. The ad model is arithmetically hopeless on Indian traffic. Lumikai measures Indian rewarded-ad eCPM at $2.43 and aggregate eCPM at $1.10, against US rewarded video at $15–28. Your own seeded ad table is unintentionally honest: $715 across 185,000 impressions. At Indian rates, one million game plays earns roughly ₹1.45–3.2 lakh gross, before the publisher's share. You will not reach one million plays for years.
  3. The category you have entered has no winners at your scale. FACEIT — a top-three global platform owned by Saudi PIF — books £11.1m/year in subscriptions, a line that grew 2.8% while the company lost £60m. Challonge is the only tournament platform on earth publishing a subscription price, and it is $12/month. start.gg ran for ten years with no take rate at all and only introduced one in February 2025 when Microsoft stopped subsidising it. Selling software to tournament organisers is not a business.
And yet — there is something real here

Three facts sit next to each other. India has 48,246 colleges and 4.5 crore students (AISHE 2023-24). Organised college esports currently reaches roughly 100–150 of them — under 0.3% penetration. And KRAFTON (128-college tour) and UniPin (50+ universities in 2026) are already spending marketing money to reach exactly those students.

The money in Indian gaming is not in players' pockets and it is not in ad impressions. It is in brand and publisher budgets, and in commerce. That is not my opinion — it is what the two working Indian pivots did. Rooter went from streaming to selling game currency and vouchers: ₹82 crore FY25, +120%, losses down 63%. NODWIN does events and owned IP: ₹658 crore FY26, first EBITDA profit, IPO-track. Loco tried to monetise attention and sold for $65m against a $150m peak.

The business hidden inside PlayCarys is campus tournament operations, sold to the people who already have a budget to reach students. The software is not the product. The software is what lets one person run fifty tournaments instead of two.

2 · Wall one — the law

The statutory text that governs you

FACT "online money game" — "an online game, irrespective of whether such game is based on skill, chance, or both, played by a user by paying fees, depositing money or other stakes in expectation of winning which entails monetary and other enrichment in return of money or other stakes; but shall not include any e-sports."

FACT "other stakes" — "anything recognised as equivalent or convertible to money and includes credits, coins, token or objects… whether it is real or virtual, which is purchased by paying money directly or by indirect means…"

Note two things. "Paying fees" is a separately enumerated trigger — Parliament deliberately wrote fees into the prohibition alongside stakes. And "indirect means" is the drafters explicitly anticipating exactly the money → virtual currency → entry chain your Revenue Model proposes.

Is the esports exemption a way out? No.

The esports definition has six cumulative limbs. Limb (v) genuinely permits registration fees and performance-based prize money. But limb (iii) requires the game to be "duly recognised under the National Sports Governance Act, 2025, and registered with the Authority." Commentators who tell you "esports can charge entry fees" are quoting limb (v) and dropping limb (iii).

FACT NSGA recognition runs through National Sports Federations, and esports has no undisputed international federation — IESF and GEF compete, and ESFI, SOGF and FEAI all contest the single Indian slot. Legal commentary describes it as a stalemate. MeitY's Minister of State has confirmed games offered as esports must obtain NSGA recognition and register with OGAI, and that determinations are per-game and per-provider, and are not precedent.

Practical translation: you would need federation-level recognition, for each third-party game you did not write, through a body that does not yet functionally exist. That door is closed.

Three compliance obligations that apply even to a free platform

The 2026 Rules dropped mandatory registration for non-monetised games — you do not need to file with OGAI upfront. But these apply regardless: age-gating and parental controls; a grievance officer with a two-tier mechanism (15 days initial, 30 days final); and data localisation of traffic data and metadata within India. That last one quietly rules out hosting your database in Falkenstein.

Tax, briefly

  • FACT Non-money games, esports and social games: 18% GST on the platform fee. Money games: 40% on the full deposit. You want to be firmly in the first bucket.
  • FACT GST registration threshold is ₹20 lakh for services — but there is a trap. A platform where third-party publishers list and you collect payment likely makes you an electronic commerce operator under s.2(45), and s.24(x) compels registration regardless of turnover. Ask a CA about this specifically before you take publisher money.
  • FACT A student can run this as a sole proprietorship — own PAN, personal savings account, no incorporation needed. Razorpay onboards individuals. You need PAN, address proof, bank account, and a site with Terms, Privacy and Refund pages.
  • FACT Any cash prize triggers Section 194BA: 30% TDS with no threshold, deducted by the platform. One more reason to have sponsors pay winners directly.

6 · The recommended business

The single strategy

PlayCarys is campus and community esports infrastructure for India, monetised by the brands and publishers who want to reach those players — not by the players themselves.

You run free-to-enter tournaments for colleges, gaming cafés and Discord communities. Players never pay to compete. The software makes one operator capable of running fifty concurrent events. The revenue is a sponsor paying for verified reach, a café paying for footfall, and a publisher paying for a campus activation they currently run with spreadsheets and a WhatsApp group.

QuestionAnswer
Who is the customer?Not the player. The brand/publisher marketing manager with a campus budget, and the gaming café owner with empty Tuesdays
What is free, forever?Playing. Entering tournaments. Creating an account. Friends, chat, profile, match history, leaderboards, achievements. Everything a student touches
What is paid?Running a branded event. Venue tooling. Cosmetics. Ad-free Pro. Later: publisher analytics
Why would they pay?Because the alternative is a spreadsheet, a WhatsApp group and no proof it worked. You sell them the report as much as the tournament
Why would they keep paying?Because the audience is yours, not theirs. Once 200 colleges run their league on PlayCarys, that reach cannot be bought anywhere else
What makes them upgrade?A café that ran one successful tournament wants a season. A brand that got one campus wants twenty

What this means for the product you have

  • Kill the entry-fee schema changes in your Revenue Model section 03. Do not add entry_fee_minor, rake_pct or the prize distribution table. That is the illegal model.
  • Keep Carys earned-only. Do not build the inward valve (buying Carys). The outward valve — spending earned Carys on cosmetics — is legal, free, and makes 30 seeded items real. Build only that.
  • Add an organisation layer (§12). This is the one significant new thing to build, and it is what turns a website into a product you can sell.
  • Add sponsor-facing reporting: participants, colleges reached, matches played, hours watched, demographics. The report is the product. You have all this data already and show none of it.

The critical funnel insight

Step 6 on the left feeds step 6 on the right. Every player who becomes an organiser adds a college to your reach map, and the reach map is what the brand is buying. Your growth engine and your revenue engine are the same mechanism — that is the only genuinely good structural property this business has, and everything should be built to strengthen it.

On "₹1 crore+" — the honest answer

₹1 crore per year is a realistic 3-year target on this plan, and it is a genuinely good outcome for a solo student founder. ₹1 crore per month is ₹12 crore a year and it is not reachable from here on this model. NODWIN took a decade and an events business to reach ₹658 crore. Rooter needed 85 million users to reach ₹82 crore.

If ₹1 crore/month is the goal, this is the wrong business and you should know that now rather than in year three. The path to that number runs through commerce (Rooter's ₹2,400/user/month on game currency) or owned IP events (NODWIN) — not through tournament software.

A Cloudflare rule that will bite you if you miss it

FACT Cloudflare's Service-Specific Terms state you must use paid Developer Platform services (R2, Images, Stream) to serve "video and other large files" via the CDN, and they reserve the right to disable accounts serving "a disproportionate percentage of… large files." Cloudflare Pages also caps a single file at 25 MiB. Game bundles must go to R2 from day one — not behind the free CDN.

Stage B → 10,000 users · ₹880–2,200/month

Trigger: your own measurement. Your Scalability doc says a match row is 239 bytes and 10,000 matches ships 2.3 MB per page load. That is the migration trigger — not a user count. When mean page payload passes ~500 KB, execute Phase 1 (stop shipping the whole database) and Phase 2 (Postgres).

  • Neon Launch (usage-based, $0.106/CU-hour) or PlanetScale PS-5 at $5/month
  • Data localisation: the 2026 Rules require traffic data and metadata stored in India. Neon/Supabase Mumbai region, or Oracle Hyderabad. Not Hetzner Falkenstein.
  • Still no Redis. Still one process. Still no queue.

Stage C → 100,000 users · ₹4,400–13,200/month

Trigger: you need a second backend process — either for uptime during deploys, or because one process is saturating. Only then does Redis earn its place, because that is the moment session and matchmaking state must leave process memory (your Phase 3).

  • Upstash Redis: 500K commands/month free, then $0.20/100K. 10M ops/month = $20.
  • Two to four Node processes behind a load balancer
  • Structured logging and error tracking (Sentry free tier)

Stage D — 1M+ · Do not plan this

You will have a team, revenue and different constraints. Any architecture you design for this today will be wrong.

You do NOT need these — and here is precisely why
KubernetesSolves multi-service orchestration across many machines. You have one service. It would add a failure mode you debug alone at 2am
MicroservicesSolves multiple teams deploying independently. You are one person. It converts function calls into network calls that can fail
KafkaSolves millions of events/sec with replay. You have 134 match results total
Redis (before Stage C)Solves shared state across processes. You have one process. Until you have two, it is a second thing that can go down
Sharding / read replicasSolves a database that cannot fit or keep up. Postgres on one box handles this workload past a million users
GraphQLSolves many clients needing different shapes. You have one web client

One schema decision to make before the first rupee

Your seeded transactions store 4.99 as a float. 4.99 has no exact binary representation. Money must be BIGINT minor units (paise) or SQL DECIMAL. Your Revenue Model already flags this — it is right, and it is the cheapest thing on this entire list to fix. Also: every seeded transaction has status completed. Real payments fail, pend, and get refunded. A schema with one state cannot record reality.

24 · The VC verdict

Would I invest? No.

Not because the work is bad — the publisher pipeline and approval-binds-to-build design are better engineering than most seed-stage products I would see. I would pass because the market cannot produce a venture return.

The arithmetic a VC does

  • Indian esports direct revenue: $22M FY25, growing to ~$132M in five years (BITKRAFT/Redseer). The entire addressable market is smaller than one mid-size Series B.
  • Tournament software has a proven ceiling: FACEIT's subscription line is £11m and flat. Challonge charges $12.
  • A venture fund needs a $1B+ outcome from its winners. To return a fund, PlayCarys would need to be roughly 10× the entire current Indian esports market.
  • The moat is relationships and operations — real, but it does not scale superlinearly, which is exactly what venture requires.

Could it become…

What would change my mind

  1. Evidence that colleges are a repeatable, self-serve channel — 50 organisations onboarded without you in the room. That would suggest something with real leverage.
  2. A commerce line that works — if you found that Indian students buy game currency through you the way they do through Rooter (₹2,400/month/user), that is a genuinely bigger business than tournaments. Test this early; it is the one thing that could make me wrong about the ceiling.
  3. Publishers paying for campus reach at scale — if KRAFTON or Krafton-scale budgets ran through your infrastructure, you are an ad-tech company with a much larger TAM.
The better direction, if you want the bigger business

Follow Rooter, not Challonge. The largest verified revenue acceleration in Indian gaming right now is selling things to gamers — currency, vouchers, gift cards — at ₹2,400/user/month, not selling tournaments or attention. You already have the two hard pieces: a player identity system and a closed virtual currency. If you tested "can I sell BGMI UC / Valorant points to my tournament players" and it worked, that is a materially larger company than the one this report describes — and the tournaments become the customer acquisition channel rather than the product.

I would test that in parallel from month three. It is cheap to test and it is the only path here to a ₹10 crore+ outcome.
